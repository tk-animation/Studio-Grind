## Before you begin

I wanted to keep it short, this guide is a resource for those who seek directions and had a tough time deciding and building a routine, and want to practice and get better at drawing, with or without the intention of becoming a good animator.

I could spend thousands of hours researching and writing a full book, but that's not my intention. This is not a mandatory curriculum at all.

I believe what makes a good art LEARNER is the ability to analyze your weaknesses and work on them through your whole life, being able to judge your own technical growth while practicing, always meta-thinking about WHAT WORKS and WHY is your biggest strength. You have to aim to be not only a good artist, but a good LEARNER.

I truly believe that if you "Max level" dynamic drawing, perspective, anatomy, gesture, rendering, light and shadow knowledge, you can probably become a really good artist.

You pick whatever you want. It's a small map so we can have fun together. 

You are 100% free to dislike it and hate the ideas, I am not that smart but my advice is probably valuable to some.
## **Intentional practice is MANDATORY, only reading will not get you far.**

You should gather good references for every step. Looking at art and building your own taste and judgement for aesthetic and technicality is a huge strength.

Talking and discussing with a community can bring a lot of new knowledge to your daily activities too. You can even consider that part of grind in this simple method.
